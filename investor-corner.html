Risk Profiler
12 questions · ~4 minutes · Used by our advisors with every new client
0 / 12
Question 1 of 12
What is your primary financial goal for this investment?
Think about what you're ultimately trying to achieve — not just in monetary terms, but in life terms.
A
Preserve my capital — I cannot afford to lose any of it
Safety is the absolute priority
B
Generate a steady income stream from my investments
Regular payouts matter more than growth
C
Grow my wealth steadily over time, accepting some ups and downs
Balanced between growth and stability
D
Maximise long-term wealth — I'm comfortable with significant short-term volatility
Growth is the priority, safety is secondary
Question 2 of 12
When do you expect to need this money?
The longer your investment horizon, the more short-term volatility you can typically absorb.
A
Within 1–2 years
Very short term — near-zero risk tolerance
B
3–5 years
Medium term — some flexibility
C
6–10 years
Long term — good capacity for volatility
D
10+ years / I may never need to sell
Full equity horizon possible
Question 3 of 12
Your portfolio drops 25% in a month due to market conditions. What do you do?
This is not hypothetical — equity markets routinely fall this much. Your gut reaction here is highly predictive.
A
Sell everything immediately — I can't sleep with these losses
Capital protection over growth
B
Sell a portion to reduce risk and monitor closely
Cautious, risk-averse response
C
Do nothing — I know markets recover and stay invested
Disciplined, long-term mindset
D
Add more money — corrections are buying opportunities
Aggressive, conviction-driven investor
Question 4 of 12
What percentage of your monthly income can you invest consistently without affecting your lifestyle?
Consistent investing matters far more than the amount. This question helps us understand your financial breathing room.
A
Less than 5% — my expenses are high relative to income
B
5–10% — comfortable but not much more
C
10–20% — I live below my means intentionally
D
More than 20% — I have significant investable surplus
Question 5 of 12
How stable is your income?
Income stability directly affects how much market risk is appropriate. A volatile income + volatile portfolio is a dangerous combination.
A
Very unpredictable — freelance, business, or contract-based income
B
Somewhat variable — performance-linked or seasonal component
C
Mostly stable — salaried with occasional bonuses
D
Very stable — government, PSU, or fixed monthly income
Question 6 of 12
Do you have an emergency fund covering at least 6 months of expenses?
Investing without an emergency fund forces you to liquidate at the worst time — when markets are down. This is one of the most important financial decisions you can make.
A
No — I live paycheck to paycheck
B
Partially — I have 1–3 months covered
C
Yes — 6 months covered in liquid savings
D
Yes — 12+ months, fully liquid and separate from investments
Question 7 of 12
What is your current experience with equity / stock market investing?
Experience shapes expectations. First-time investors often underestimate their emotional reaction to losses.
A
None — I have only kept money in FDs, savings accounts, or gold
B
Limited — I've invested in mutual funds or stocks but briefly
C
Moderate — I've stayed invested through at least one market cycle
D
Experienced — I understand volatility and have a long track record
Question 8 of 12
You have ₹10 lakhs to invest today. Markets have fallen 30% in the last 3 months. What do you do?
Deploying capital during fear is when the best returns are made — and also when most investors freeze.
A
Wait for markets to recover before investing
I need to see stability first
B
Invest a small amount now and wait with the rest
Cautious, staged approach
C
Invest in equal instalments over 6 months via STP
Systematic and disciplined
D
Invest the full amount now — falling markets are an opportunity
High conviction, long horizon
Question 9 of 12
What return do you realistically expect from your investments over the long term?
Unrealistic expectations cause poor decisions. Understanding what is historically achievable is critical.
A
6–7% — roughly what FDs offer
I'm happy if I beat inflation slightly
B
8–10% — steady, reasonable equity-like returns
Realistic for well-diversified portfolios
C
12–15% — I'm targeting above-average long-term compounding
Achievable with disciplined equity investing
D
20%+ — I expect to significantly beat the market
Possible but requires high risk and expertise
Question 10 of 12
How would you describe your attitude toward financial risk in general?
Not just investments — how do you approach risk in life decisions: career changes, business, insurance, health decisions?
A
Very cautious — I always choose the safer option, even at a cost
B
Somewhat cautious — I take calculated risks only when necessary
C
Comfortable with risk — I weigh upside and downside and decide
D
Risk seeker — higher risk often means higher reward and I embrace that
Question 11 of 12
Do you have dependants relying on your income (spouse, children, elderly parents)?
Financial dependants significantly change risk capacity — losing capital isn't just about you.
A
Yes — multiple dependants, all heavily reliant on me
B
Yes — one or two dependants, partially reliant on my income
C
Minimal — my partner earns independently, dependants are few
D
None — I am fully financially independent with no dependants
Question 12 of 12
If you had to choose between these two investments for ₹5 lakhs, which would you prefer?
This final question reveals your true preference when faced with a real trade-off — not a hypothetical one.
A
Fixed Deposit at 7% — guaranteed, no risk
₹5L becomes ₹7L in 5 years, guaranteed
B
Debt Mutual Fund at ~8% — low risk, slightly better returns
Minor fluctuations, mostly stable
C
Balanced Fund at ~11% — moderate risk and growth
Some volatility, meaningful upside
D
Equity Fund at ~13–15% — high risk, high potential
Could double in 5 years, or fall 40% first

This is your financial shock absorber. Without it, any crisis forces you to liquidate investments — often at a loss.

Less than 1 month
🔴 Critical gap — top priority to fix
B
1–3 months
🟡 Below ideal — needs improvement
C
3–6 months
🟢 Getting there — acceptable minimum
D
6+ months in a separate liquid account
✅ Excellent — financial foundation is solid
Pillar 1 of 5 — Emergency Fund · Question 2 of 10
Where is your emergency fund kept?
Liquidity matters as much as the amount. Money locked in FDs or equity is not truly available in an emergency.
A
I don't have one yet
B
Fixed Deposits — some penalty to break
C
Savings account or sweep-in FD
D
Liquid/overnight mutual fund — instantly redeemable
Earning returns while staying accessible
Pillar 2 of 5 — Insurance · Question 3 of 10
Do you have adequate life insurance to protect your family if you were to pass away unexpectedly?
The standard recommendation is 10–15x your annual income in pure term cover — not investment-linked policies like ULIPs or endowment plans.
A
No life insurance at all
🔴 Serious risk to your family
B
Yes — but only through an employer group policy
🟡 Will lapse if you change jobs
C
Yes — a personal term plan, but cover may be insufficient
🟡 Good start, check if 10x income
D
Yes — a pure term plan with cover of 10x+ annual income
✅ Well protected
Pillar 2 of 5 — Insurance · Question 4 of 10
Do you have comprehensive health insurance that covers hospitalisation costs?
A single hospitalisation can set back years of savings. Company health insurance may not be sufficient for serious illnesses or post-retirement needs.
A
No health insurance of any kind
🔴 One hospitalisation could wipe out savings
B
Only company-provided health insurance
🟡 Vulnerable when you leave the job
C
Personal health policy for self only
🟡 Family members still exposed
D
Personal family floater policy with adequate coverage (₹10L+)
✅ Well protected
Pillar 3 of 5 — Debt · Question 5 of 10
What percentage of your monthly income goes toward EMIs and loan repayments?
Above 40% is a financial red zone. High debt repayment leaves little room for savings, emergencies, or wealth building.
A
More than 50% — debt is consuming my income
🔴 Urgent attention needed
B
30–50% — manageable but tight
🟡 Limiting investment capacity
C
15–30% — mainly home loan, well-managed
🟢 Healthy range for productive debt
D
Less than 15% or no debt at all
✅ Strong — maximum surplus to invest
Pillar 3 of 5 — Debt · Question 6 of 10
Do you carry a credit card balance month to month (i.e., pay less than the full amount due)?
Credit card debt typically charges 36–42% annually — the highest-cost debt that exists. No investment can reliably beat this.
A
Yes — I regularly carry a significant balance
🔴 Clear this before investing anything
B
Sometimes — in a difficult month I carry a partial balance
C
Rarely — only in genuine emergencies
D
Never — I always pay the full outstanding amount
✅ Excellent discipline
Pillar 4 of 5 — Savings Rate · Question 7 of 10
What percentage of your post-tax income do you save or invest each month?
The single most powerful lever in wealth creation is your savings rate — more than asset allocation, fund selection, or timing.
A
Less than 5% — barely anything is left at month end
B
5–15% — I save whatever is left over
🟡 Reactive saving — try automating it
C
15–25% — I pay myself first before spending
🟢 On track
D
More than 25% — wealth creation is a conscious priority
✅ Excellent financial discipline
Pillar 4 of 5 — Savings Rate · Question 8 of 10
Do you have a written or clearly defined monthly budget?
You can't manage what you don't measure. A budget doesn't restrict — it gives you clarity on what you can afford to invest.
A
No — I spend freely and hope something is left
B
I have a rough idea in my head but nothing written
C
I track expenses and review them monthly
D
Yes — detailed budget, automated investments on salary day
✅ Best practice
Pillar 5 of 5 — Investments · Question 9 of 10
Are you currently investing regularly in any market-linked product (mutual funds, equity, NPS)?
Regular, automated investing removes emotion from the equation and harnesses compounding effectively.
A
No — all my savings are in FDs, gold, or a savings account
B
Occasionally — I invest lump sums when I have spare cash
C
Yes — I have active SIPs or regular contributions
🟢 Good habit
D
Yes — diversified SIPs + NPS + review my portfolio quarterly
